The challenge is care home's low net profit with around the clock resident care makes it hard for care home owners to make a living solely by running the business. with projected elderly population of 85 years and older more than doubling next 20 years, where do they go when they need affordable care homes? 



Economic concepts

Net profit \~$35k-$60k for a 2-bed home vs. an RN's hospital pay of $136,320 ($65.54/hr)(BLS/DLIR) (opportunity cost; incentives) which discourages registered nurses from entering the field of care home business.  It simply does not make economic sense. Care homes keep people out of costlier nursing homes (positive externality; public savings) however, if care home owners have to earn supplement income from another employment and healthcare benefits who's taking care of residents. GET charged on gross receipts, not profit (Tax burden on a low-margin business) According to care home analysis it can be from $3885 (10.9% of  net profit of $35,411) to $5005 (8.4% of net profit of $59,828) which is significant portion of care home owner's income.

I am recommending the GET exemption because 10.9% of net profit for small business owner is big portion of added profit.  It can be argue that amount is not significant enough, but it is a great starting point into other measures that follow.  Act 47 added the exemption as HRS §237-24.3(12), effective January 1, 2026. However, the guidance lists eligible providers but doesn’t define “health care facility” or name care homes.  Therefore, I recommending Legislature amends §237-24.3(12) to name community care foster family homes, ARCHs, and E-ARCHs explicitly.  With that said, the objection will be the lost state tax revenue which is approximately $5,850,000 (1,300 homes X $4,500).  On the other hand, if Medicaid pays for a Level 2 resident going to a nursing home instead due to lack of availability of care homes, it will roughly cost $8500 to $10,200 for a month about $101,000–$123,000 a year according to Hawaii's Medicaid State Plan Amendment H-23-0014, effective January 1, 2024.  Compared to the care home for the same Level 2 is about $45,100 a year which costs roughly $56,000–$78,000 less per year than in a nursing home.  Which then lost state tax revenue of $5,850,000 divided by$56,000 lower of rough estimate of extra cost for higher cost nursing homes, comes out to 104.  The state will break even on the tax revenue lose if 104 residents remain at the care homes. One thing to consider is though Medicaid costs are shared between federal and state governments so that state need more than 104 residents to remain at care home to break-even.
