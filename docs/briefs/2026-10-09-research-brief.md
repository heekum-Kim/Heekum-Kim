# Research Brief — Care Home Supply in Hawaii

## The challenge -- Medicaid has the set payment rate, and the care home owners have no input in payment with two bed homes making net profit of approximately $35,000 to $60,000 a year depending on patient payment mix, is it a sustainable business for most of care home owners or do they need second employment to supplement their income and medical insurance?  is there  something we can do to alleviate the difficulty and make running care homes more profitable and sustainable? I have a few nurse friends doing a care home as a side gig because it's just extra income, their main source of income is from their registered nurse salary from a major hospital where base pay is about $150,000 yearly.

## Economic concepts-- Micro issues are Medicaid applies to the state being the dominant buyer who sets an administered price set by a monopsony buyer   and net profit of approximately $35,000 to $60,000 a year is opportunity cost and incentives.  Also Micro and tax related issues are Medicaid care payments excluded from income (IRS Notice 2014-7); Act 47 GET exemption and GET charged on gross receipts, not profit. Both Micro and Macro issue is care homes keep people out of more expensive nursing home which is positive externality with public savings that government will profit from keep care homes sustainable.  last but not least Macro issue is Hawaii's aging population with higher demand for care homes with limited labor supply both on number of available care homes and caregivers.  With better medical care and extended life expectancy demands will only go up but where is the supply of labor for that demand?  This is particularly time sensitive issue, like compound interest the problem gets exponentially bigger as time passes. Growing demand verses dwindling supply which need to addressed today.

## Planned analysis--closer look at Hawaii's care home status to verify the estimates, Medicaid payment rates and how it is excluded from income and its unintended trade-offs which is lowering their income for Social Security credits and limits on Solo 401(k) contributions. From the data, analyze which policy change makes most sense for both care home owners and for government in public savings.  Aging population against the low supply of care home is the main problem i will focus on.  I will use the Med-QUEST rate memo, the state CSV of homes, CDC age data, and other statistics on Hawaii’s aging population. My policy options are raising Level 2 Medicaid rates, extending the GET exemption explicitly to care homes, and letting difficulty-of-care payments count toward self-employed retirement plans.
