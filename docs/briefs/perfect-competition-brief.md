1-1.1	Engagement Brief Perfect Competition

My farm is deciding what crops to produce to maximize the profit for 36-week season.  Since the price is already decided by the market, I must make a good choice on the crop mix among tomatoes, carrots and mesclun.

Parameters are 36 weeks in length, fixed costs of $20,000, total 64 beds, my labor : $50,000/season, half my time in the field (720 field hours, \~$34.72/hr. implied) and temporary workers: up to 4, $25,000 each, 1,440 hours each (\~$17.36/hr. implied)  

Crop options: Tomatoes 20 max bed, price $8,800 per bed, 2.50 labor hours a week per bed, $880 of fertilizer per bed.  Carrots 20 max bed, price $2094 per bed, 0.833 labor hours a week per bed, $440 of fertilizer per bed.  Mesclun 30 max bed, price $2700 per bed, 1.25 labor hours a week per bed, $880 of fertilizer per bed. 

Here is the kicker.  Tomatoes have 10.00%  diminishing returns per bed due to compound labor cost and carrots at 2.5% and mesclun at 1.25% respectively.

Just by looking at the crop chart with price per bed, labor intensity and fertilizer cost, I am roughly guessing that carrots are more profitable than tomatoes or mesclun because of less labor and fertilizer cost.  At first, I  thought tomatoes would bring most profit per bed even with most labor intensive:  however, diminishing return/labor compound at 10 percent per bed, I’m less inclined to plant them. Tomatoes with 10 percent labor compound penalty with highest labor cost will be very expensive, which is not cost effective.  For that reason, I am guessing the optimal balanced mix to be carrots 20, mesclun 30 and tomatoes 14.  With my rudimentary math, tomatoes make 7900 for the first bed, with ten percent penalty second bed will be 7100, third 6300, fourth 5500 and so forth.  I considered lowering the tomatoes bed to 11 because the whole point is to make more profit instead of using maximum allotted plots. However, I will stay with tomatoes 14 beds because my calculations after 11 or 12 beds returns will be less or marginal, but I'm not certain it will be more beneficial than leaving 3 beds empty for the season.  I am willing to take the risk of 3 extra beds of tomatoes if it’s at least marginally profitable instead of eating up my profit.  It will be interesting to see if my prediction is correct or completely off that I should have planted more tomatoes.
