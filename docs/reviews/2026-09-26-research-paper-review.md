You asked whether this is a doable research project. Yes, and a good one. Choosing between a sole proprietorship, an LLC and an S-corp election is a real decision with real money in it, and your first paragraph already has the insight most people miss: an LLC on its own "changes almost nothing on the tax bill." Your question, "at what profit level does each option make sense," is the right shape because it asks for a number.

**Frame it as a break-even profit.** The S-corp election saves self-employment tax on profit not paid as salary, but adds payroll, a reasonable salary, extra filings and state fees. The savings grow with profit while most of those costs are fixed, so the two lines cross. That crossover is your answer, and a chart of after-tax income against profit for each option, with the crossover marked, is your figure.

**Find the Hawaiʻi costs, because they move the crossover.** Your question ends with "what does it cost to run in Hawaii?" List the state fees and filings for each option. Then check whether Hawaiʻi's employer requirements, such as temporary disability insurance and the Prepaid Health Care Act, apply to an owner's salary from their own S-corp. If they do, the break-even sits higher than national guides suggest.

**Write what would prove you wrong.** For example: if the Hawaiʻi break-even is above what most one-person businesses earn, the answer for most readers is "stay simple."

**In order:**

1. Write the break-even framing and sketch the after-tax-income chart.
2. List the Hawaiʻi fees and filings, and answer the TDI and Prepaid Health Care question.
3. Write the result that would prove you wrong.
